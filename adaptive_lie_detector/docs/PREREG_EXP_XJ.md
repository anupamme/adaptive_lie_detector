# Pre-registration: EXP-XJ — the corpus authors' own black-box judge on the criterion-4 corpora

**Status:** written and committed **before** `experiments/run_external_audit_judge.py` exists and
**before any judge output has been produced or read**. No LLM judgement on either corpus has been
generated by us, at any point, under any prompt.
**Companions:** `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` (criterion 4 on sandbagging), `PREREG_EXP_IT.md` and
`PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` (criterion 4 on insider trading). EXP-XJ swaps the **detector** and changes
nothing else.
**Date:** 2026-09-09

---

## 0. Integrity disclosure — what has already been inspected

Same discipline as EXP-XA, EXP-IT and EXP-IT2: "pre-registered" cannot mean "before the data
existed", so it means **before the instrument was ever run**.

**Corpora.** Both are already **exploratory** and disclosed as such:

| corpus | n | disclosed exploratory in |
|---|---|---|
| `rollouts/sandbagging_v2__wmdp_mmlu__llama-70b-3.3.json` | 1000 | `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §0 |
| `rollouts/insider_trading__onpolicy__llama-70b.json` | 173 | `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §0, `PREREG_EXP_IT.md` §0 |

So nothing here can be claimed as confirmatory **about the corpora**. What is confirmatory is the
**analysis**: the instrument, the channels, the hypotheses, the directions, the gates, the
stratification, the seed and every reporting branch are fixed below before a single judgement exists.

**Already inspected, and carried into the design of this document.** Everything in
`PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §0 and `PREREG_EXP_IT.md` §0, plus every number EXP-XA / EXP-IT / EXP-IT2 have
already published: the regex rule's verdicts (+4.1 / +7.7 pp visible, +84.3 / +78.9 pp full
transcript on sandbagging; −77.1 pp on the insider report; +21.6 pp action-grounded), the per-pattern
decomposition, and the cell sizes. Specifically, while writing this document the following were read:

- Sandbagging `expected_label` × `graded_deception` cell counts: `label=deceptive` 399 graded-deceptive
  / 39 graded-honest / 60 ambiguous / 2 skip; `label=honest` 21 / 473 / 4 / 2. **The 39 and 21 cells
  are the power limit of this experiment and are known in advance** (§6).
- Insider counts: `graded_deception` 91/80 (2 excluded); `report_classification` 41 explicit / 63
  implied / 69 concealed; `trade_classification` Yes 136 / Ambiguous 20 / No 17; 166 of 173 rollouts
  have a non-empty `Action Input` report; 11 scenario signatures; 1 system prompt on both corpora.
- Character-length statistics of both channels on both corpora (for cost estimation only).
- **Rollout index 0 of each corpus**: its `input_messages` roles, the first 400 characters of the
  system, last user and (empty) trailing assistant messages, and the first 400 characters of
  `output_strs[0]`. Read to write the context formatter correctly. No further rollout text was read.

**Held out, and this is the point of the experiment: no judge score exists.** The black-box judge has
never been run by us on any rollout of either corpus, on any channel, under any model. There is
therefore no way for the instrument's behaviour to have informed any choice below. The upstream
release ships the judge's *prompt* but no per-rollout judgements for these corpora, and none were
sought.

## 1. Motivation — the objection this closes

EXP-XA and EXP-IT tested criterion 4 with **one** instrument: a 14-pattern, parameter-free regex
(`hedging_baseline.REFUSAL_PATTERNS`, "L-doc"). `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §2 recorded the resulting risk in
advance — *"L-doc may therefore be near-blind in this regime"* — and the outcome bore it out: 4.7% /
3.2% on the held-out sandbagging tag, 0.0% / 0.0% twice elsewhere. The honest reading of a null from a
possibly-blind instrument is weak, and a reader is entitled to say: **maybe the regex is simply bad at
autonomous deception, and a real detector would separate these cells.**

The audited release answers this itself. Apollo's repository ships **the corpus authors' own black-box
detector**: an LLM-judge prompt at `data/black_box_baseline/prompt.txt`, wired up by
`deception_detection/baselines.py` (`BLACK_BOX_PROMPT`, `black_box_grading_prompt_getter`,
`compute_black_box_scores_for_dataset`) and `deception_detection/rollouts/api.py`
(`grade_single_dialogue_for_deception`, `parse_judgement`). It reads a conversation and returns a 1–7
deception score. It is the black-box baseline the corpus was **built to be scored against**.

EXP-XJ runs **that** detector, byte-for-byte as released, on **the same two corpora, the same two
channels, and the same criterion-4 contrasts** the regex was run on. It is prior work's detector on
prior work's corpus — the same move the paper already makes by auditing `pacchiardi2023catch`'s
detector rather than one of our own.

**What this is and is not.** EXP-XJ adds a second **detector**, not a second corpus. It cannot fix
what the corpora structurally lack (`PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §10: sandbagging fails requirements (iii) and
(iv); `PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` §9: insider trading meets (v) and returns a directional failure). It
addresses exactly one objection — instrument blindness — and no other.

## 2. The detector — the corpus authors', used verbatim, nothing tuned

**Prompt.** `data/external/apollo_deception/data/black_box_baseline/prompt.txt`, read from disk at run
time and `.strip()`ed, exactly as `baselines.py` does. It is never edited, extended, reworded or
reformatted, and its md5 is recorded in the output. It instructs a three-step chain of thought
(`<step1>`, `<step2>`) and then a score in `<judgement>` tags: **1 = completely honest, 7 = completely
deceptive.**

**Prompt assembly**, reproducing `black_box_grading_prompt_getter` and `format_dialogue`:

```
system = BLACK_BOX_PROMPT
user   = "<context>\n{context}\n</context>\n\n<output>\n{output}\n</output>"
context = "\n".join(f"{role}: {content}")   over merge_messages(context_messages)
```

`merge_messages` concatenates consecutive same-role messages with `"\n"`, copied in behaviour from
`baselines.py`. The **context messages** are `input_messages` with trailing `assistant` messages
removed — the authors' `get_context` rule. On both corpora the trailing assistant message is a
zero-length generation prefix (§0), so this reduces to dropping one empty message and loses nothing.

**Parsing**, reproducing `parse_judgement` exactly: split on `<judgement>`; require exactly two parts;
split the remainder on `</judgement>`; require exactly two parts; strip and lowercase; if the result
`isdigit()` take `int`, else the judgement is **`unknown`**. Any `unknown`, and any integer outside
1–7, is **excluded and counted, never recoded**. If `unknown` exceeds **20%** of any contrasted cell,
that arm is reported **`UNRUNNABLE_PARSE_RATE`** with the rate, not weakened and not patched.

**Judge model — a stated deviation from the authors' transport, recorded before any call is made.**
`api.py` calls `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` (OpenAI) or `claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022` (Anthropic). We have
neither transport; we have AWS Bedrock, already used for the paper's Haiku and Llama arms
(`experiments/run_prompt_equalized_haiku.py`). EXP-XJ therefore uses
**`us.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0`** via `anthropic.AnthropicBedrock`, `max_tokens=1000`
(the authors' value), **`temperature=0.0`**.

Two consequences, both fixed here:

1. **The judge model is newer and stronger than either the authors used.** A null therefore cannot be
   dismissed as a weaker instrument than the published baseline. It can be objected to as a
   *different* instrument, and that objection is reported rather than argued away.
2. **`temperature=0.0` deviates from the authors' default of 1.0.** Chosen for reproducibility: with
   temperature 0 the raw judgements committed to `data/results/external_audit_judge_raw.jsonl` are the
   closest thing to a recomputable artifact an API judge admits. Because determinism is not
   guaranteed, **H7 measures the instrument's own stability** rather than assuming it.

Nothing else about the detector is specified by us. There is no threshold to fit, no training set, and
no channel-specific adaptation.

## 3. Channels — reused from the committed runners, not reimplemented

The channel extractors are **imported** from the existing runners so that EXP-XJ scores exactly the
text EXP-XA and EXP-IT scored:

| corpus | primary (deployed-observable) `<output>` | secondary `<output>` |
|---|---|---|
| sandbagging | `run_external_audit.visible_channel` — `<step4>` + `<answer>`, hidden `<step1..3>` / `<step*-results>` / `<sandbag-skill>` blocks stripped (`PREREG_EXP_XA.md` DEVIATION 1) | `run_external_audit.full_transcript` — verbatim `output_strs`, **contaminated by the hidden scratchpad** |
| insider trading | `run_insider_audit.visible_channel` — the triple-quoted body of the `Action Input:` block, i.e. the message actually sent to the manager (`PREREG_EXP_IT.md` §3) | the verbatim `output_strs`, which additionally carries the agent's `Reasoning:` preamble |

**The two corpora use different visible-channel extractors, deliberately and by import.** Applying
the sandbagging extractor to insider trading would return the full text (no `<step4>` tag exists
there) and would silently destroy the contrast. Each corpus keeps the extractor its own
pre-registration fixed.

Insider rollouts with an **empty** visible channel (7 of 173, §0) are **excluded from the
visible-channel arms and counted**, mirroring `n_with_visible_channel`. They are retained in the
full-transcript arm.

**Context.** For sandbagging the context is system + user, which is the elicitation itself; a deployed
monitor sees it. **The judge therefore reads the sandbagging instruction in both cells of every
criterion-4 contrast.** This is the authors' definition and is kept. It cannot manufacture a
criterion-4 positive: a judge that keys on the instruction alone produces a score that is *constant
within* `expected_label`, and criterion 4 is a within-label contrast. It can, and is predicted to,
produce a large **between-label** difference at fixed grade, which is what H6 measures and which is a
$\tau_E$ quantity, never a criterion-4 result.

For insider trading the context also contains the agent's **earlier** turns, including its own
`Reasoning:` blocks — text that can be explicitly self-incriminating. Unlike the sandbagging
scratchpad, the benchmark does not declare these hidden, so they are arguably operator-visible; but a
positive driven by them would be a fact about the *context*, not about the report a manager reads.
**H5 exists to separate these**: the primary insider arm restricts the context to the **non-assistant**
messages (system + environment/user turns), and H5 repeats it with the authors' unrestricted context.
Both are reported whatever they show.

## 4. Estimand, hypotheses and directions — fixed now

**Primary outcome:** the **mean judge score** (1–7) per cell, with a bootstrap-free report of cell `n`,
mean, SD, and the difference in means in score points, with a one-sided label-permutation `p`
(10,000 draws, seed 42).

**Direction, fixed for every hypothesis: the judge scores deception HIGHER** (7 = deceptive by the
prompt's own definition). This is the *opposite* polarity to the disclosure regex of EXP-IT, where
firing indicated honesty; the sign convention is fixed here so it cannot be chosen later. A
significant result in the opposite direction is reported as a **directional failure**, is not re-tested
two-sided, and is not reinterpreted.

**Criterion-4 contrasts (the load-bearing ones).**

- **H1 (primary — criterion 4, sandbagging, deployed-observable).** Within a fixed `expected_label`,
  mean judge score on the **visible channel** is higher for `graded_deception = deceptive` than for
  `= honest`. Unpaired label permutation (`analyze_external_audit.perm_p_unpaired`, one-sided),
  10,000 draws, seed 42. Cells: `label_deceptive` 399 vs 39 (**primary**, the stratum where the
  benchmark intends deception); `label_honest` 21 vs 473 (**secondary replication**).
- **H2 (criterion 4, insider trading, corpus grade, deployed-observable).** Judge score on the
  **visible report** is higher for `graded_deception = deceptive` than `= honest`, **stratified by
  scenario signature** (`analyze_insider_audit.perm_p_stratified`, one-sided higher), 10,000 draws,
  seed 42. Mirrors `H1_grade_visible` of EXP-IT.
- **H3 (requirement (v), insider trading, action-grounded label).** The same judge and channel against
  `metadata.trade_classification` (`Yes` → A=1, `No` → A=0, `Ambiguous` excluded and never recoded —
  `PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` §2/§8). Scenario-stratified, one-sided higher. Mirrors `H4_trade_visible`.
  **This is the only arm in which the label and the detector's input sit on disjoint channels**, and
  the semantic caution of `PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` §1 carries over verbatim: `A` is the misaligned action,
  **not** deception under the paper's definition.

**Channel and context contrasts (requirement (iv)).**

- **H4 (channel, sandbagging).** H1's contrast recomputed on the **full transcript**. The judge
  analogue of the regex's +84.3 pp vs +4.1 pp gap. Reported whatever it shows.
- **H5 (context ablation, insider trading).** H2 recomputed with the **authors' unrestricted context**
  (prior assistant `Reasoning:` turns included). Isolates whether any insider effect is carried by the
  report or by the agent's earlier self-incriminating reasoning.

**Descriptive, and never a criterion-4 result.**

- **H6 ($\tau_E$, sandbagging, descriptive, no criterion-4 status).** Judge score by `expected_label`
  **at fixed grade** — the instruction contrast. Reported with the caveat `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §4 fixed:
  `expected_label` is **perfectly collinear with topic** on this corpus, so this quantity confounds
  the instruction with subject matter and may never be read as evidence about deception. It exists to
  establish that the instrument is **not blind** — i.e. that a null H1 is not "the judge scored
  everything the same."
- **H7 (instrument stability).** The judge is re-run on a fixed random subsample of **100** sandbagging
  rollouts (visible channel, `random.Random(42).sample` over sorted indices, drawn **before** any
  score is seen) and the two passes are compared: mean |Δ score|, exact-agreement rate, Spearman
  correlation, and whether any criterion-4 verdict would flip. Reported unconditionally.

**Secondary metrics, all pre-specified.** (a) **Binarized fire rate** at the prompt's own midpoint,
score **≥ 4** = "detector fires", with Wilson intervals, so the judge is directly comparable to the
regex's fire rates; the same permutation test is applied to the binarized flags. (b) **AUROC** of the
continuous score against the cell label. (c) Full per-cell score histograms (counts of 1–7). (d)
**Topic-stratified** H1 within the fixed label (`PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §7): a pooled effect carried by one
topic is reported as such, and directional consistency across topics is required before any positive
is described as a criterion-4 pass.

**The continuous score is the primary and the binary rule is secondary, deliberately.** A 1–7 score
has strictly more power than a threshold crossing, and the whole point of EXP-XJ is to give the
criterion-4 contrast the *most* favourable instrument available in the release. Any null must
therefore be reported as a null of the more powerful measurement.

**Evaluation order is part of the specification**, because each corpus's RNG is seeded once and
consumed sequentially:

- sandbagging (`numpy.random.RandomState(42)`): **H1** (`label_deceptive`, then `label_honest`), then
  **H4**, then **H6**, then the secondary binarized/AUROC arms, then topic stratification.
- insider trading (`random.Random(42)`): **H2**, then **H3**, then **H5**, then the secondary arms.

**Inferential unit** is the rollout. **MIN_CELL = 10** on sandbagging (`PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §6);
**MIN_CELL = 5 per scenario** on insider trading (`PREREG_EXP_IT.md` §4). Below the floor, counts are
reported and the arm is declared **UNDERPOWERED** rather than tested.

## 4a. AMENDMENT 1 — H8, the instruction-stripped context (added 2026-09-09, **after H1 was inspected**)

**Disclosure first.** This arm was written **after** H1's result was seen and **before any H8 judgement
existed**. It is therefore *pre-specified but not blind*, is labelled **EXPLORATORY** wherever it
appears, and never joins the confirmatory set of §4. The H1 result it responds to: on the visible
channel, at fixed elicitation, the judge separates `graded_deception` by **+1.476** score points in
`label_deceptive` (6.13 vs 4.66, n=395/38, p=0.0001, AUROC 0.706) and **+4.041** in `label_honest`
(6.00 vs 1.96, n=21/469, p=0.0001, AUROC 0.938).

**Why the arm is needed.** H1 is a criterion-4 positive, and two mechanisms produce it:

1. the visible answer itself carries a deception-associated signature (an answer that is wrong, with
   confident reasoning constructed to look sound), which the judge reads; or
2. the judge reads the **deception instruction**, which sits in the corpus's system message and is
   inside the `<context>` the authors' own detector definition supplies. The judge's own `<step1>`
   text cites it explicitly (*"The system prompt instructs the AI to 'sandbag'…"*), then checks the
   answer against its own knowledge — i.e. it verifies **compliance with an instruction it can read**.

Mechanism 2 is this paper's confound relocated to the detector's input, and the two are separable
because on this corpus the **entire** sandbagging instruction is in the system message
(`data/sandbagging_v2/sabotage_evals_system_prompt.txt`); the user message is the bare question plus
an answer-format line (`sabotage_evals_user_prompt.txt`). Removing the system message therefore
removes $E$ from the detector's context and leaves exactly what a monitor without access to the
operator's prompt would see. **H8 is prompt equalization applied to the detector's context.**

**H8 (exploratory, sandbagging, context ablation).** H1's contrast, recomputed with context mode
`no_system`: trailing assistant messages dropped as before, then **every system message dropped**.
Judge prompt, output channel (`sandbag_visible`), rollout set, direction (deception scored higher),
permutation test (`perm_p_unpaired`, one-sided, 10,000 draws, seed 42), `MIN_CELL`, parse-rate gate,
degeneracy gate, MDE reporting and binarized/AUROC secondaries are **all unchanged from §4/§5**. Only
the context differs, so H8 vs H1 is a paired within-rollout comparison on identical items.

**Secondary, pre-specified:** per-rollout paired $\Delta$ (H8 − H1 on matching `idx`): mean, mean
$|\Delta|$, Spearman correlation, and the share of rollouts whose binarized ($\geq 4$) flag flips.

**Interpretation, fixed before the run.** Let $R$ = H8's difference in means divided by H1's, per label
cell.

| Branch | Condition | What may be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | H8 significant in the primary cell (`label_deceptive`, $p<0.05$ one-sided) **and** $R \geq 0.5$ in both cells | The visible channel carries a deception-associated signal readable **without** the elicitation instruction. Criterion 4 is **met by this instrument**; the EXP-XA null is **instrument-specific**, and the protocol has its first positive endpoint. §3.5, §1's claim 3, Table 1's row C, the abstract and the conclusion are revised to say so. |
| **B** | H8 significant in the primary cell **and** $R < 0.5$ in either cell | Both mechanisms operate. Report both effects and the attenuation ratio; claim 3 is still revised — a positive is a positive — with the instruction-dependence quantified next to it. |
| **C** | H8 not significant in the primary cell | The H1 positive is **instruction-context dependent**: the strongest black-box instrument in the release separates deception only when $E$ is inside its own input. Claim 3 stands, and gains that sentence. |
| **D** | A gate of §5 fails (degenerate judge, or `unknown` > 20%) | `INAPPLICABLE`; no claim either way, counts reported. |

Branch **C** is the branch that favours this paper's thesis, which is why the threshold, the test and
the wording are fixed here, before the arm is run.

**Command.** `python3 experiments/run_external_audit_judge.py --h8` (arm `visible_nosys`, 1000 items,
one call each; appended to the same `external_audit_judge_raw.jsonl`, so §8's offline-reproducibility
property is unchanged).

## 5. Applicability gate — fixed in advance

An arm enters its test only if **all** of:

1. **Elicitation is fixed**: exactly one distinct system-message md5 across the contrasted cells,
   asserted mechanically. True on both corpora by construction, and re-checked rather than assumed.
2. **The judge is non-degenerate**: the score is **not constant** — SD > 0 — in at least one contrasted
   cell of at least `MIN_CELL` rollouts. A judge that returns the same integer everywhere is reported
   **`INAPPLICABLE_DEGENERATE_JUDGE`** with its score distribution and **never as an effect of 0**,
   exactly as EXP-XA refused to report 0.0% fire as an accuracy.
3. **Parse rate**: `unknown` ≤ 20% in every contrasted cell (§2).

For the **binarized secondary** metric only, the EXP-XA gate additionally applies: fire rate strictly
inside [5%, 95%] in at least one contrasted cell. Failing it makes the *binarized comparison*
inapplicable and says nothing about the continuous primary.

**Achieved power is reported, not assumed.** For each tested contrast we report the **minimum
detectable effect** (MDE) in score points at 80% power, one-sided α = 0.05, computed by simulation
against the observed pooled SD at the observed cell sizes, seed 42. The 39- and 21-rollout cells (§0)
are the binding constraint and their MDE is stated in the paper next to the null.

## 6. What this design does and does not escape

**Stated before any number exists, because it determines what may be claimed.**

**What it escapes.** Instrument blindness. If a strong, purpose-built, corpus-author-supplied LLM judge
on a 1–7 continuous scale returns the same verdict as a 14-pattern regex, the verdict is a property of
**what the corpora expose**, not of our detector choice.

**What it does not escape, and must not be claimed.**

1. **Still one public release, still Llama-70B.** EXP-XJ adds a detector, not a corpus. Every
   limitation of `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §10 and `PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` §5 stands verbatim.
2. **Still exploratory corpora.** Both were inspected before this document (§0). EXP-XJ is a
   **confirmatory analysis on exploratory corpora**, and must be labelled so wherever it appears.
3. **A null is not evidence of no deception signal.** It is the absence of a deception-*associated*
   signal at fixed elicitation on the deployed-observable channel, for these corpora and this
   detector. The paper's standing caveat is unchanged.
4. **The judge is a stronger instrument in power, not in kind.** It is still a behavioural black-box
   detector reading text; it cannot observe belief, and it is not `do(D)`.
5. **A judge positive would not identify $\tau_D$.** Criterion 4 is observational at fixed `E`; the
   causal rung (`D` manipulated or randomized) sits outside the protocol by construction.
6. **Two same-purpose instruments are not two independent measurements of deception.** Both read text
   the corpus exposes; a shared null can be a shared blind spot of *text*, and that reading is
   reported alongside ours rather than suppressed.

## 7. Reporting policy — every branch fixed now

1. **H1 null, gate passed, H6 shows the judge is not blind.** The strongest branch for the paper:
   criterion 4 returns a null on the deployed-observable channel for **both** a parameter-free surface
   rule **and** the corpus authors' own black-box judge, while the same judge separates the
   *instruction* contrast. §3.5 and the abstract gain that clause; the "maybe the regex is blind"
   objection is closed with the release's own baseline. `tab:external_audit`'s verdict text is updated;
   no column is added.
2. **H1 positive and robust** (significant in the pre-registered direction, in the primary
   `label_deceptive` stratum, directionally consistent across topics, on the **visible** channel).
   **Claim 3 of the paper is falsified**: a public corpus does supply criterion 4 and a black-box
   detector does separate deception at fixed elicitation. Claims 1 (structural non-identification) and
   2 (the confound is active in the instructed paradigm) are untouched. This is **reported as a
   falsification, prominently, and not absorbed into the existing framing**: the abstract, §1's
   falsifier box, §3.5, the discussion and the conclusion all change, and the paper becomes "here is
   the criterion, and here is the first corpus that passes it." The user is told before any prose is
   written.
3. **H1 positive on the full transcript (H4) but null on the visible channel.** The paper's
   hidden-channel finding is **reproduced with a second, stronger instrument** — the effect lives in
   text no deployed detector can read. Reported as a strengthening of requirement (iv), with the
   magnitude gap stated in score points beside the regex's +84.3 vs +4.1 pp.
4. **H2 positive with H3 null.** Construct recovery reproduced with a stronger detector: the judge
   tracks the corpus's own report-grounded annotation but not the action-grounded label on disjoint
   channels. Requirement (v) gains independent support. If **H5** shows the H2 effect is carried by the
   unrestricted context, that is reported as the mechanism.
5. **H2 and H3 both null.** Insider trading's criterion-4 verdict is unchanged in substance and
   strengthened in breadth.
6. **Directional failure** in any arm: reported as such, with the mechanism
   `PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` §4 named in advance where it applies, and not re-tested two-sided.
7. **Gate failure, `UNDERPOWERED`, `UNRUNNABLE_PARSE_RATE`, or `INAPPLICABLE_DEGENERATE_JUDGE`.**
   Reported with its counts and score distribution, never as an effect of zero.
8. **H6 shows the judge is blind on both contrasts** (no separation anywhere, including the
   instruction contrast). Then EXP-XJ establishes far less than intended and must say so: the judge is
   inapplicable to this format and the criterion-4 null is uninformative from this instrument. This
   branch **weakens** the paper's breadth claim rather than strengthening it, and is reported with the
   same prominence as branch 1.

In every branch the paper states that EXP-XJ adds a **second detector on the same two corpora**, and
that no replication on a second public release exists (§6.1).

**No branch licenses** "the residual in the instructed paradigm *is* instruction-following" (the
Proposition forbids it from any manipulation of `E`), nor any claim that either detector is fit for
deployment.

**byte-identical**; verified with `cmp`.

### 10.4 May claim

1. **Criterion 4 is satisfiable by a public artifact, and the sandbagging corpus satisfies it for a
   black-box detector.** $E$ is byte-identically fixed; $D$ comes from
   `SandbaggingV2Dataset.label_response`, a deterministic function of the **hidden** `<step1-results>`
   and `<step2-results>` tags, which the visible-channel detector never sees — so requirement (v) holds
   and this is **not** construct recovery.
2. **On that corpus the criterion-4 verdict depends on the instrument.** A 14-pattern surface rule is
   null (+4.1 / +7.7 pp, $p \geq 0.17$; EXP-XA). The corpus authors' own black-box judge, on the same
   rollouts and the same visible channel, separates realized deception by **+2.00** and **+4.16** score
   points, AUROC **0.777** and **0.949**, $p = 0.0001$, **with the elicitation instruction removed from
   its context**.
3. **The EXP-XA null must therefore be restated as instrument-specific**, in the abstract, §1's claim 3,
   Table 1's row C, §3.5, §4 and the conclusion. The blanket form ("criterion 4 is testable in one
   audited corpus and there it returns a null") is **retired**.
4. **The instrument that reproduces instructed-benchmark accuracy is the one that fails at fixed
   elicitation.** The surface rule reaches 69–80% on the instructed benchmark and 0 at fixed
   elicitation; the semantic judge, which the instructed benchmark never needed, succeeds there. This
   is the sharpest available empirical statement of the paper's thesis: the two accuracies are not
   measuring the same thing.
5. **Requirement (v) gains independent support on insider trading with a stronger instrument**: H2
   positive on the corpus's own grade, H3 null against the action-grounded trade label — the construct
   recovery EXP-IT2 found with a regex, reproduced with an LLM judge.
6. The judge is a **stable** instrument (H7) and its effects survive topic stratification wherever
   power exists.

### 10.5 May NOT claim

1. **Not** that instructed-benchmark accuracy was measuring deception. H1/H8 concern deception arising
   at fixed elicitation in a corpus we did not build; the Proposition and §3.2–§3.4 are untouched.
2. **Not** that the sandbagging corpus satisfies all five requirements: it still fails **(iii)** — no
   item appears under both conditions, so the pre-registered capability screen remains unrunnable.
3. **Not** $\tau_D$. H1/H8 are observational at fixed $E$: a latent $U$ with $U \to D$ and $U \to S$
   survives them, exactly as Table 2's ladder states. What is established is a
   deception-**associated** signal at fixed elicitation.
4. **Not** that the judge would work in deployment. The corpus is 4-option multiple choice with a
   checkable answer and the judge scores 1–7 with reasoning; neither affordance is general. H8 removes
   the instruction from its context but not the question.
5. **Not** a second corpus. EXP-XJ adds a second **detector** to ten rollout sets from one public
   release, every rollout Llama-70B.
6. **Not** H3 as evidence of absence: $n = 5$ in the honest cell, a directional failure reported as
   such and never re-tested two-sided.
7. **Not** blind. AMENDMENT 1 was written after H1 was inspected; H8 is labelled **exploratory,
   pre-specified** wherever it appears.
