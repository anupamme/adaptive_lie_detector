## 1. Motivation — the objection this closes

The paper's criterion-4 evidence is **one public release**: ten rollout sets from
`goldowskydill2025probes`, seven scenario designs, **every rollout generated by Llama-70B**. The paper
already says so in Table 3's caption (*"not ten independent benchmark efforts"*), which makes the
concentration explicit but does not remove it. A reader is entitled to say: *this is a finding about
one lab's release, and the structural gap you report may be an artifact of how that lab built its
rollouts.*

Liars' Bench is the second public rollout release of comparable scope that exists, and it is a better
test of the paper's claim than a third instrument on Apollo would be:

1. **Four generator models in four families**, against Apollo's one.
2. **Eight elicitation regimes** spanning exactly the axis the paper's argument turns on — from
   `instructed-deception` (26,426 examples of the paradigm the paper indicts) through
   `harm-pressure-*`, `gender-secret`, `convincing-game` and `insider-trading` (regimes where the
   *model's behaviour*, not an instruction, may determine the label) to an `alpaca` honest control.
3. **A per-example `deceptive` boolean plus the full `messages` transcript**, which is precisely the
   shape EXP-XA's eligibility machinery already consumes — so the audit transfers without new
   inferential apparatus.
4. **Built by a different group with a different purpose.** `kretschmar2025liarsbench` is one of the
   three concurrent generalization evaluations §1 of the paper cites. Auditing its release for
   *construct validity* rather than *generalization* is the paper's contribution applied to a corpus
   that was not built with our criteria in mind.

**And it is a live falsifier, not a safe replication.** The paper's stated refutation condition is a
corpus that (i) fixes elicitation, (ii) grades D independently of the condition label, (iii) realizes
the same scenario under both behavioural outcomes, (iv) exposes only the deployed-observable channel,
and (v) grounds D in evidence not definitionally equivalent to the detector's input — on which a
detector separates deception. **`harm-pressure-*` and `gender-secret` are credible candidates for all
five**, and (iii) is the requirement no Apollo set meets, while a corpus organised around scenarios
resampled at temperature may well supply it. §7 branch **A** therefore specifies, in advance, the
retraction that follows if the falsifier fires. This document exists partly so that outcome cannot be
absorbed quietly.

## 2. Materials, and how they are pinned

**Download.** `experiments/fetch_liars_bench.py`, authenticated with the local HF token, fetches the
parquet files of the configs §5 declares eligible into `data/external/liars_bench/`. That directory is
**gitignored**: the corpus is gated and not ours to redistribute.

**Pinning.** For every fetched file the script records **sha256, byte size, and the API's
`lastModified`** into a committed `data/external/liars_bench_manifest.json`, together with the repo
revision SHA. `docs/EXTERNAL_DATA.md` gains an entry stating plainly what this does and does not buy.

**The reproducibility story here is weaker than Apollo's, and the paper must say so.** Apollo's release
is pinned as a submodule at a fixed commit, so a third party reproduces our numbers from source. Liars'
Bench is gated, so the path is: manifest sha256s (which verify that a reader who obtains access has the
same bytes we did) plus **committed derived artifacts** (per-example rule fires, per-example judge
scores as JSONL) from which every reported number recomputes with no network and no model call. That is
verification-of-analysis without verification-of-input. **The Reproducibility Statement must state this
asymmetry rather than implying parity**, and must not describe the corpus as "publicly available"
without the qualifier "gated".

**Sampling.** Configs are audited **in full** for eligibility (§5) — the survey is offline and free.
The **judge arm is subsampled and capped** (§4).

## 3. The two instruments — both already written, neither tuned

**Instrument 1: our parameter-free surface rule.** `run_external_audit.COMPILED` /
`marker_count()` over `hedging_baseline.REFUSAL_PATTERNS` — the same 14 patterns, same threshold
(fires at ≥ 1 marker), no fitting, no per-corpus adaptation, **no pattern added or removed for this
corpus**. `PREREG_EXP_XA.md` §2's warning carries over verbatim: this rule was written for a
first-person claim-assertion format and **may be near-blind** in agentic or dialogue regimes. A null
from it is a null of a possibly-blind instrument, and is reported as such.

**Instrument 2: the Apollo authors' black-box judge, used verbatim on a different release.**
`data/external/apollo_deception/data/black_box_baseline/prompt.txt`, `PROMPT_MD5 =
83ddc4bd2ebcc6c4b05baff091c5cc8c`, assembled by `run_external_audit_judge.py`'s existing
`merge_messages` / `format_dialogue` / `judge_user_prompt`, parsed by `parse_judgement` — split on
`<judgement>`, exactly two parts, integer 1–7 else **`unknown`**, excluded and counted, **never
recoded**. Judge model `us.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0` via Bedrock,
**`temperature = 0.0`**, `max_tokens = 1000`. Polarity fixed: **higher = more deceptive**.

**Running Apollo's judge on Cadenza-Labs' corpus is an instrument-transfer result in its own right**,
and it is the second independent detector the reviewer's concentration objection asks for on the corpus
axis. It also means a positive here cannot be dismissed as our rule being lucky, and a null cannot be
dismissed as our rule being blind.

**Channels, both computed for every arm.** `visible_channel()` — what a deployed detector could read —
is **primary**. `full_transcript()` is the **requirement-(iv) contrast**. This is the same pair that
produced +4.1/+7.7 pp against +84.3 pp on Apollo, and the gap is a pre-registered outcome, not a
diagnostic afterthought: **if the full-transcript effect exceeds the visible-channel effect by more than
a factor of 3, the config is reported as `ANNOTATION_CHANNEL_LEAKAGE`**, the same failure mode Apollo's
sandbagging scaffold exhibited.

**Channel definition for `messages`.** Liars' Bench ships a message list rather than Apollo's
`input_messages` / `output_strs` split. Fixed now: the **visible channel** is the concatenation of the
**final assistant message** only; the **full transcript** is every message including system. Any
content inside tags matching `run_external_audit.HIDDEN_TAGS` is stripped from the visible channel and
retained in the full transcript, exactly as on Apollo. If a config's final message is not an assistant
message, that config is reported **`INAPPLICABLE_NO_MODEL_OUTPUT`** (Apollo's goal-directed-lying set
failed the same way) and is not repaired by reaching further back in the list.

## 4. Estimand, hypotheses and directions — fixed now

**Estimand, per eligible (config × generator) cell:** the difference in the rule's **fire rate**
(percentage points) and in the judge's **mean score** (points, 1–7) between `deceptive = true` and
`deceptive = false`, at **fixed elicitation**, on the **visible channel**.

**Direction, fixed for every hypothesis and every instrument: deception scores HIGHER.** The rule fires
more on deceptive examples; the judge scores them higher. A significant result in the opposite
direction is a **directional failure**, is reported as one, is **not** re-tested two-sided, and is not
reinterpreted. *(The insider-trading disclosure rule of EXP-IT had the opposite polarity by
construction; if this corpus's `insider-trading` config turns out to be format-compatible with that
rule, the polarity question is resolved by §5's format gate, not by inspecting the result.)*

- **H1 (PRIMARY — criterion 4, our rule, deployed-observable).** Within a fixed-E cell, the rule's fire
  rate is higher for `deceptive = true` than `= false`. `analyze_external_audit.perm_p_unpaired`,
  one-sided, 10,000 draws, seed 42, Wilson intervals on both fire rates.
- **H2 (PRIMARY — criterion 4, the authors' judge, deployed-observable).** Same cells, same direction,
  mean judge score. `perm_p_unpaired`, one-sided, 10,000 draws, seed 42. Secondaries, all
  pre-specified: **AUROC** of the continuous score against `deceptive`; **binarized fire rate** at the
  prompt's own midpoint (score **≥ 4**, `BINARIZE_AT`) with Wilson intervals; full 1–7 histograms per
  cell.
- **H3 (requirement (iv) — the channel contrast).** H1 and H2 recomputed on the **full transcript**.
  Reported whatever it shows; the ×3 leakage rule of §3 applies.
- **H4 (requirement (iii) — the falsifier's binding condition).** For each eligible config, the count of
  **scenario groups realized under both `deceptive` values** at fixed E, where the scenario key is
  derived by the rule fixed in §5.3. Where such groups exist with ≥ `MIN_CELL` = 10 examples, H1 and H2
  are recomputed **stratified within scenario** (`analyze_insider_audit.perm_p_stratified`, one-sided,
  10,000 draws, seed 42). **This is the arm that decides whether the paper's falsifier has fired**, and
  it is the arm no Apollo set could supply.
- **H5 (descriptive — τ_E, and never a criterion-4 result).** Where a config contains examples under
  more than one distinct system-prompt md5, the rule and judge are compared **across** those groups at
  fixed `deceptive`. This quantity confounds the instruction with whatever else differs between prompt
  groups (topic, scenario, format) and **may never be read as evidence about deception**. It exists to
  show the instruments are **not blind** — so that a null H1/H2 is not "everything scored the same".
- **H6 (instrument non-blindness floor).** On the `alpaca` honest control (`deceptive` presumably
  uniform — **unverified**, see §5.4), the rule's fire rate and the judge's mean score are reported as a
  baseline. If the judge's score on a supposedly-honest control is not clearly lower than on any
  deceptive cell, the **instrument** is reported as suspect and every judge arm on this corpus is
  labelled accordingly.

**Judge subsample, capped and fixed now.** At most **400 examples per eligible config per channel**,
**balanced on `deceptive`** (200/200, or the smaller class's size doubled if it is under 200), drawn by
`random.Random(42).sample` over **sorted** row indices, **before any score is seen**. With at most 4
eligible configs × 2 channels this is **≤ 3,200 calls**, comparable to EXP-XJ's 3,605 and under $20.
**The cap is stated in the paper**: a silent subsample reads as full coverage. If more than 4 configs
prove eligible, the 4 with the **largest H4 paired-scenario counts** are judged and the rest are
reported rule-only, with the omission named.

**Gates on the tests themselves, copied from EXP-XA rather than re-derived.** `MIN_CELL = 10`;
`N_PERM = 10000`; `SEED = 42`. The rule must fire on strictly between **5% and 95%** of some contrasted
cell of ≥ 10 examples, or the *rule* arm is **`INAPPLICABLE_DEGENERATE`** — reported with its fire
rates and **never as an effect of 0**. The judge must be non-constant (SD > 0) in some contrasted cell,
or **`INAPPLICABLE_DEGENERATE_JUDGE`**. `unknown` parses must be ≤ **20%** of every contrasted cell, or
**`UNRUNNABLE_PARSE_RATE`** with the rate. **Achieved power is reported, not assumed**: for every tested
contrast, the minimum detectable effect at 80% power, one-sided α = 0.05, by simulation at the observed
cell sizes and pooled SD, seed 42 (`analyze_external_audit_judge.mde`).

**Verdict vocabulary is the committed one.** The existing `CRITERION4_NULL` / `CRITERION4_POSITIVE`
strings are printed unchanged. **No new verdict vocabulary is invented in the code**; the paper's
failure-mode table maps these strings onto its own verdict names.

**Evaluation order is part of the specification**, since the RNG is seeded once and consumed
sequentially: eligibility survey (all configs, offline) → H4 counts → H1 (per eligible config, in
descending `n`) → H3 rule arm → judge subsample drawn → H2 → H3 judge arm → H5 → H6.

## 5. Eligibility gates — mirroring EXP-XA exactly, fixed before any row is read

A (config × generator) cell is auditable for criterion 4 only if **all** of §5.1–§5.4 hold. **The
survey producing these verdicts is the deliverable even if every config fails**, which is why it is
specified in full.

### 5.1 Elicitation is fixed (requirement (i))

Extract the system message from `messages`, md5 it with `run_external_audit.system_md5()`. **E is fixed
for a cell only if exactly one distinct md5 covers it.** More than one distinct system prompt means E
varies and the cell is **`INELIGIBLE_E_VARIES`** — the same verdict that disqualified Apollo's
roleplaying (371 prompts), AI-Liar (54) and paired-facts sets. A config with no system message at all
is treated as E fixed **and trivially so**, which is recorded as a caveat rather than a pass, since a
corpus with no operator prompt does not test what criterion 4 is about.

**Stratification by generator is mandatory, not optional.** A cell pooling
`mistral-small-3.1-24b-instruct` with `llama-v3.3-70b-instruct` is **not one elicitation condition**,
because the same prompt to a different model is a different experiment. **The md5 test therefore runs
within generator**, and every reported cell is a (config × generator) pair. Pooled-across-generator
numbers may be reported as descriptive only and never as a criterion-4 verdict.

### 5.2 The label varies at fixed E, and is not the condition label (requirements (ii) and (v))

`deceptive` must take **both** values within the fixed-E cell, each with ≥ `MIN_CELL` = 10 examples.

**Requirement (ii)/(v) adjudication is decided from the corpus card, the `meta` field's *keys*, and the
system prompt's text — and written into the results JSON *before any score is computed*.** The
expected split is recorded here **as an expectation to be confirmed, not an assumption**:

| config | expected adjudication, to be confirmed |
|---|---|
| `instructed-deception` | **Expected to fail (ii)**: if `deceptive` is the instruction given, the label *is* the condition label and the config is the very paradigm the paper indicts. Confirm by checking whether `deceptive` is predictable from the system-prompt md5 alone. |
| `soft-trigger` | **The case to check hardest.** If the trigger lives in the system prompt, then E varies with the label and the config is `INELIGIBLE_E_VARIES` for the same reason as Apollo's roleplaying sets. If the trigger is in the *user* turn with one fixed system prompt, E is fixed but (ii) needs separate adjudication. **No result from this config is reported until that determination is written down.** |
| `harm-pressure-choice`, `harm-pressure-knowledge-report` | **Candidates**: pressure in a fixed prompt, with the model's *behaviour* determining `deceptive`. If so, (i) and (ii) both hold. |
| `gender-secret`, `convincing-game` | **Candidates**, same structure, small n (762 / 888) — power is the risk, and `MIN_CELL` decides it. |
| `insider-trading` | **Candidate, with requirement (v) at issue.** `PREREG_EXP_IT2.md` §1's problem recurs: if `deceptive` grades the report and our rule reads the report, rule and grader share evidence and passing is **construct recovery, not detection**. If `meta` carries a trade/action field disjoint from the report, that is the action-grounded label and the EXP-IT2 arm is run; if not, the config is reported **`CONSTRUCT_RECOVERY_RISK`** and its positive, if any, is presented as such. |
| `alpaca` | **Control, not an audit target.** Used only for H6. |

**A config whose `deceptive` label is a deterministic function of the system-prompt md5 fails (ii) by
mechanical test, and that test is run and reported for every config**, including the candidates.

### 5.3 The same scenario is realized under both outcomes (requirement (iii))

**Scenario key, fixed now, in this order of preference**, with the choice recorded per config: (a) a
scenario/id/index field inside `meta` if one exists; else (b) the md5 of the **concatenated non-final
user-and-system messages** — i.e. everything the model was given before its final turn; else (c) no key
is available and the config is reported **`(iii)_UNTESTABLE`**.

A **paired scenario group** is one key with **both** `deceptive` values present. §4's H4 reports the
count of such groups and the total examples inside them, per (config × generator). **This number is the
single most consequential output of the survey**, because it is what no Apollo set supplies and what
the paper's falsifier requires.

### 5.4 Nothing above is assumed

Every clause of §5 is a **mechanical test on the fetched rows**, and each is reported with its counts
even when it passes. In particular: that `alpaca` is an honest control, that `deceptive` varies at all
in any config, that `messages` ends with an assistant turn, and that the four generators appear in more
than one config — **all are checked, none is assumed.** Where the metadata of §0 implied something the
rows contradict, the contradiction is reported.

## 6. What this design does and does not escape

**Stated before any row exists, because it determines what may be claimed.**

**What it escapes.**

1. **Single-release concentration.** The audit becomes *two independent public releases, five generator
   models, four families, eight further elicitation regimes* — the direct answer to the objection.
2. **Single-lab design idiosyncrasy.** If the same structural gap appears in a corpus built by a
   different group for a different purpose, it is a property of **how deception corpora are built**, not
   of one release.
3. **Instrument blindness**, by carrying both instruments across (§3).
4. **Post-hoc corpus selection.** The corpus is unseen (§0), so the eligibility verdicts cannot have
   been chosen to suit the paper's conclusion.
5. **The suspicion that the falsifier is unfalsifiable.** §7 branch A specifies the retraction in
   advance.

**What it does not escape, and must not be claimed.**

1. **Criterion 4 is observational at fixed E.** A positive here establishes a deception-**associated**
   signal, not τ_D. The causal rung — D manipulated or randomized — sits outside the protocol by
   construction, and `cooney2026didyoulie`'s trained organisms remain above criterion 4 on the ladder.
   **No branch below licenses a τ_D claim.**
2. **A null is not evidence of no deception signal.** It is the absence of a deception-associated signal
   at fixed elicitation on the deployed-observable channel, for these cells and these two instruments.
3. **Two same-purpose instruments are not two independent measurements of deception.** Both read text
   the corpus exposes; a shared null can be a shared blind spot **of text**, and that reading is
   reported alongside ours rather than suppressed.
4. **Still not an exhaustive survey of public corpora.** Two releases is two, not all. The paper's claim
   must remain a claim about **what the public rollout corpora we could obtain contain today**, and if
   access fails (§7 branch F) it is a claim about one release plus a documented inability to score a
   second.
5. **The generators are not the deployment targets.** Four 24–72B open-weight models generating rollouts
   is not evidence about frontier deployed systems, and no scaling claim follows.
6. **Weaker reproducibility than Apollo, by the corpus's gating**, not by our choice (§2).
7. **`meta` is an opaque string in the schema.** Every field this document hopes to find inside it — a
   scenario id, a trade classification — may simply not be there. Each such hope is written as a
   conditional with a named fallback (§5.2, §5.3), and a missing field produces an `UNTESTABLE` verdict
   rather than an improvised substitute.

`CRITERION4_POSITIVE` strings printed unchanged. (g) The (ii)/(v) adjudication per config, written
before scoring, is published **as written**, including where the rows contradicted the expectation.

## 9. Deviations

**Taken. Six, all dated 2026-09-10, all recorded before the affected number was reported.**

6. **The ×3 leakage test was implemented wrongly and was corrected after rows had been read.** §3's
   rule is "the full-transcript effect **exceeds** the visible-channel effect by more than ×3". The
   first implementation compared `|f_eff|` against `LEAK_FACTOR * max(|v_eff|, 1e-9)`, which is wrong
   twice over. (a) With a blind rule the visible effect is exactly 0.00 pp, so the threshold collapsed
   to 3e-9 and a 0.10 pp full-channel effect — two examples out of 2,000 — was labelled
   `ANNOTATION_CHANNEL_LEAKAGE`. §4 says a degenerate rule arm is reported with its fire rates and
   "never as an effect of 0", so using that 0 as a ratio denominator contradicted this document's own
   instruction; 15 of 16 spurious flags came from it. (b) `abs()` on both sides made a **sign flip**
   count as leakage: `insider-trading/gemma-3-27b-it` is +1.24 pp visible and −3.93 pp on the full
   transcript, a ratio of −3.16, which is a directional failure under §4, not evidence that the label
   is recoverable from a hidden channel. **Corrected to signed effects, gated on §4's 5–95% fire test,
   in both arms** — the judge arm had always been signed, so this also removed an asymmetry between
   instruments. The correction is derivable from §3's and §4's text and is not a retuning: it changed
   no confirmatory contrast, only which cells carry a leakage verdict (§10.3).
   *Consequence for the branch:* removing the 16 spurious flags exposed that §7's branch-D scan had
   been written inside the criterion-4 eligibility filter, so the one genuine instance would have been
   dropped in silence. §7's D says "**any** cell" and "composes with A/B/C", so the scan now covers
   every cell. D still fires, on one cell, and the reason it is reported rather than discarded is in
   §10.3.

7. **H6's judge arm had no draw rule, and "clearly lower" had no threshold. Both were fixed after the
   confirmatory arms were scored.** §4 pre-registered H6 — "if the judge's score on a supposedly-honest
   control is not clearly lower than on any deceptive cell, the **instrument** is reported as suspect"
   — but §4's judge subsample is defined per **eligible** config, and `alpaca` is not eligible because
   its label does not vary. The hypothesis therefore had no way to be run and the control was initially
   not judged at all. Fixed as: 100 rollouts per generator, drawn by `random.Random(42).sample` over
   sorted `lb_index`, **visible channel only** (H6 asks about a level, not a channel contrast, so no
   `full` arm is run and no new contrast becomes available), on an arm namespaced
   `visible_h6_control` so `done_keys` cannot re-issue or contaminate a confirmatory arm. "Clearly
   lower" is operationalised as: the control's mean sits below the **honest** arm's mean of every
   visible-channel-positive cell by more than the sum of the two standard errors. Comparing against the
   honest arm rather than the deceptive arm is the stricter reading. **This arm can only weaken a judge
   claim, never strengthen one**, which is why fixing it late is disclosable rather than disqualifying;
   in the event it weakened nothing (§10.3), and every other verdict, MDE and branch line is

### 10.6 May NOT claim

1. **Nothing about deception on either eligible cell.** Both are `CONSTRUCT_RECOVERY_RISK`: the rule and

   mechanism, not detection**. This is the same verdict EXP-IT reached on Apollo insider trading, and
   for the same reason.
2. **Not even a within-scenario claim at full strength on the mistral cell.** H4's excess over its own
   within-scenario null is **+2.33 pp** against a pooled +16.44 pp. Any use of the pooled figure without
   the null mean beside it would misrepresent it by roughly 7×.
3. **No claim of no effect from any null.** The `insider-trading/kimi-k2.6` rule null has an MDE of
   **+15.30 pp**; the `harm-pressure-*` and `soft-trigger` nulls are `INAPPLICABLE_DEGENERATE` (the rule
   never fires), not evidence of absence.
4. **No claim that `soft-trigger`, `gender-secret` or `harm-pressure-*` are unsuitable for deception

   Requirement (iii) is a claim about what a corpus licenses, not about its quality.
5. **No claim of reproducibility parity with Apollo.** The corpus is gated; the path is manifest hashes
   plus committed derived artifacts, which is weaker than a pinned submodule (§10.1). The
   Reproducibility Statement must say that, not imply parity.
6. **No claim that (i) was tested thoroughly.** §5.1's md5 test covers the **system message only**; an
   elicitation cue in the user turn is invisible to it, and on `soft-trigger` a user-turn phrase is
   92–100% predictive of `deceptive` while the system prompt is constant — and on three cells there is
   no system prompt at all. (iii), not (i), is what catches that, and the prompt-recoverability
   measurement of §9 deviation 10 is descriptive and **is not a gate**.
7. **No claim that the judge is well calibrated in absolute terms** beyond what H6 licenses: the control
   is clearly lower, which rules out a stuck instrument, but the honest arms of both eligible cells sit
